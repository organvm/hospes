## Guest Archetype

A civic-systems designer who turns complex public processes into usable services. Operates at the
intersection of policy, interface design, and institutional mechanics. Has a public body of work —
government tooling, open-source civic infrastructure, or published methodology. Not primarily a
podcast presence; would approach the conversation as a practitioner, not a promoter.

**Why this archetype:** The show's fixed structure (Claim → Stress Test → Artifact) is well-matched
to guests who work with legibility problems — the gap between how a system is designed and how its
users experience it. A civic-systems designer lives in that gap professionally. The artifact segment
is especially natural: this guest type produces working documents as a matter of practice.

**Why now:** The C1 public-adjacency route keeps social cost low on a show that is still
establishing its track record. A practitioner who is not a habitual podcast guest is also a stronger
stress test of the format than a media-trained subject.

---

## The Claim

*Public systems fail less often from a shortage of expertise than from interfaces that conceal who
can decide, what evidence counts, and what happens next.*

---

## Stress Test

Present a fictional service whose rules are internally correct but whose user journey is opaque.
Ask the guest to identify the minimum changes that make authority, evidence, and recourse legible —
without promising that design alone can solve underlying policy conflict.

---

## Artifact

A one-page "legible decision path" template with fields for: decision owner, required evidence,
decision outcome, appeal route, deadline, and receipt. Produced in the room during the conversation.

---

## Producer Brief

- **Fixed segments:** The Claim, The Stress Test, The Artifact  
- **Rotating segment:** *Build the Worst Version* — ask the guest to describe the most opaque version
  of the fictional service they could imagine, then identify the one change that would open it  
- **Research boundary:** use only synthetic examples until claims link to public evidence  
- **Relationship boundary:** no familiarity or prior-use claim may be invented  
- **Approval gate:** relationship owner reviews any eventual outreach draft

## What This Alternate Pilot Tests

**Practitioner guest in a fixed format.** A civic-systems designer is a stronger stress test of
the Artifact segment than a media-trained guest: the object produced in the room will either be
useful or it will not, and there is no performance frame to carry a weak artifact through. This
alternate pilot answers whether the format can produce a genuinely working artifact — not a
symbolic one — with a guest who builds working documents professionally.
