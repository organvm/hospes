## Guest Archetype

A food-science founder who balances sensory appeal, manufacturing constraints, and evidence-backed
claims. Has built a consumer product that required navigating the gap between what tastes true, what
manufacturing can repeat at scale, and what evidence actually supports. Not primarily a podcast
presence; would approach the conversation as a builder who has solved a constrained problem, not as
a spokesperson.

**Why this archetype:** The claim-and-evidence structure of the show is well-matched to guests who
have had to publicly defend what their product actually does versus what they wanted it to do. A
food-science founder lives with that pressure at the intersection of regulatory requirement and
consumer communication. The Artifact segment is natural: a claim audit or evidence ladder is the
kind of working document this guest produces professionally.

**Why now:** The C2 route (prior professional touchpoint) keeps social cost lower than a cold
approach while testing a different supply channel than pilot-01's C1 public-adjacency route.
Proving the show works across C1 and C2 routes in the first slate is a structural win.

---

## The Claim

*A product should not make a public claim that its manufacturing and evidence systems cannot
repeat.*

---

## Stress Test

Present a fictional launch brief containing one attractive but unsupported benefit claim. Ask the
guest to separate sensory language, measurable product facts, and claims that require legal or
scientific review before publication. No medical or nutritional conclusion is treated as
established during the conversation.

---

## Artifact

A "claim ladder" that labels each proposed statement as one of: observation, tested product fact,
qualified inference, or prohibited claim pending evidence. Produced as a working document in the
room; not polished or captioned — the raw object and the context in which it appeared.

---

## Producer Brief

- **Fixed segments:** The Claim, The Stress Test, The Artifact  
- **Rotating segment:** *Receipts* — ask the guest to identify one claim the company made publicly
  that turned out to be harder to support than expected, and walk through what the receipt for
  supporting it actually looked like  
- **Research boundary:** synthetic product and evidence only; no real brand, product, or health claim  
- **Sensitive boundary:** any health, legal, or compensation language in a reply routes to human review; no product endorsement or personal-use claim may be inferred from participation  
- **Approval gate:** sponsor-like statements require factual and legal clearance before publication

## What This Alternate Pilot Tests

**The Artifact segment with a practitioner who produces evidence documents professionally.** The
food-science founder is uniquely positioned to produce a claim ladder that is genuinely usable —
not a demonstration — because distinguishing observable fact from qualified inference is their
daily work. This alternate pilot answers whether the Artifact can be a real-world working object
rather than a show prop, with a guest whose professional practice requires exactly that discipline.
