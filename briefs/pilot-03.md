# Pilot Brief 03 — Weak-Tie Crossover

**Status:** RESEARCHING  
**Relationship class:** C1 (second-degree connection; weak tie via shared comedy/tech/music scene)  
**Relationship owner:** Host  
**Route:** Publicist — formal inquiry via the guest's representation  
**City / Studio:** Austin — TBD conversation-room venue; touring-guest window preferred  
**Social cost:** 3 / 5 — formal publicist outreach on a show that does not yet have public numbers  
**Host effort:** NOTE (a personal note from Host to attach to the publicist inquiry)  

---

## Guest Archetype

A figure who operates fluently in two registers: comedy and technology, or comedy and music. Known
enough to have publicist representation; not so large that the ask is implausible on a new show.
Austin-based or touring through Austin on a predictable schedule. The crossover profile — funny
*and* technically serious — is the archetype the show is designed to attract and cannot reliably
find through the existing Unlicensed Therapy network alone.

**Why this person:** The show needs to prove it can land guests outside the immediate network. A
publicist route is the formal intake process; testing it on Pilot 3 means the team knows whether
it works before it matters. A crossover guest also tests whether the show's format holds when the
guest's primary identity is not "podcast guest."

**Why now:** Austin's touring circuit creates a natural batching opportunity — a guest who is
already traveling can be intercepted without requiring a dedicated trip. That logistics proof is
valuable in itself.

---

## The Claim

*Fluency in two languages is not the same as being bilingual — you still have an accent in one of them.*

A figure who works in both comedy and a technical discipline (code, music production, hardware)
lives with the permanent sense that they are a tourist in at least one of their own fields. The
conversation examines which field feels native and which feels performed — and whether that
distinction holds up under pressure.

---

## Stress Test Plan

- **Hidden audio quality:** Pilot 3 is the designated test of the room-read audio setup. The
  Austin venue will not be a controlled studio. The stress test is whether two redundant hidden
  audio channels produce a clip that is technically usable — broadcast quality is not required;
  intelligible and characterful is. A short extracted clip will serve as the proof-of-concept
  artifact for the audio approach.
- **Formal intake route:** Does the publicist pathway work? The stress test is operational: inquiry
  sent, response received, booking confirmed or declined, all within a documented timeline. Every
  step in the formal intake process is a data point for the system design.
- **Guest experience without a brief:** A publicist guest will have been prepped to some degree.
  The stress test is whether the no-brief, no-topic-list format survives contact with a guest who
  arrived expecting talking points. The room protocol must be strong enough to redirect a guest
  who starts performing rather than talking.

---

## Intended Artifact

**Hidden-audio proof clip:** A two-minute audio segment extracted from the hidden-microphone
capture — no visible mic in frame in any corresponding video. Produced as a standalone audio
file demonstrating room-read quality. This is the technical artifact; it is not necessarily
the most interesting two minutes of the conversation. Its function is to demonstrate that the
Club Random-style visual grammar does not compromise the audio record.

---

## Rotating Segment

**Claim / Stress Test / Artifact** (fixed) +  
**Rotating:** *The Translation* — midway through the conversation, the guest is asked to explain
the most technical or esoteric aspect of their secondary field to Host and Producer in terms that
a twelve-year-old could follow. The explanation is not interrupted or corrected. The hosts
respond only with questions, not observations. The segment tests whether the format can generate
genuine teaching moments without becoming educational content.

---

## City + Studio Logistics

Austin. Preferred timing is when the guest is already in Austin for a touring engagement or
festival — intercept the guest in their city rather than flying them to LA or NYC. Venue is a
conversation room in the guest's hotel, a green room, or a borrowed private space — not a
podcast studio. Two redundant hidden audio channels; no visible microphones. The visual grammar
of the recording should be consistent with the LA and NYC pilots even though the production
environment is less controlled.

---

## Relationship Route + Social Cost

Host's producer sends a formal inquiry to the guest's publicist. The inquiry is one page: who the
show is, who the hosts are, what the format is, and a personal note from Host. No media kit, no
listener numbers, no pitch deck. Social cost: 3 — this is a real ask on a new show, and the
publicist will evaluate it against the guest's existing commitments. If the publicist declines,
that is also a data point: what would make the ask easier to accept? That question feeds the
next iteration of the outreach template.

---

## What This Pilot Must Prove

**Hidden audio capture and room-read quality.** The show's visual grammar requires that
microphones are never visible in the frame. This pilot is the first test of that constraint
in an uncontrolled environment — not Host's Melrose studio, not a familiar NYC room, but a
touring venue in Austin. By the end of this recording the team will have a concrete answer
to the question: can the hidden-audio approach produce a usable record in a room we don't
control? If yes, the format is portable. If no, the technical constraint needs to change
before the show goes further.

**Guest experience in the formal route.** Every guest who comes through a publicist is a guest
who expected a different kind of show. This pilot will reveal whether the room protocol is
strong enough to convert a prepared guest into an honest participant — or whether the intake
process needs to do more work before the guest arrives.
