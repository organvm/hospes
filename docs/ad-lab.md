# Commercial Break Lab

Advertising is treated as a designed interstitial, not dead air read from a script.
The ad is clearly disclosed, factually controlled, and creatively authored. Editorial
control stays with the show.

---

## House-ads-first doctrine

Before any paid sponsor enters the format, prove the creative system with house ads:

- Ari's studio network (LA, NYC, Austin)
- The show's email list
- Another owned show in the network
- A live studio event
- Anthony's creative projects and publications

House ads allow the format to be developed, tested, and refined without a sponsor
watching nervously. The first time a paying partner sees a Commercial Break Lab slot,
it should already look finished.

## Six recurring formats

### Product on Trial

Ari cross-examines the product directly — its claims, its category, its failure modes.
Anthony constructs an absurdly overqualified defense. The final line states the product's
actual utility plainly.

Protocol: Ari is the prosecution; Anthony is the overcredentialed defense attorney;
the closing statement is simple and factual. The comedy comes from the mismatch
between the defense's intellectual machinery and the plainness of what the product
actually does.

### The Overbuilt Explanation

Anthony develops an elaborate intellectual theory of a basic product — its philosophy,
its cultural implications, its place in the history of the relevant category. Ari
reduces it to the single reason someone would buy it.

Protocol: Anthony escalates the abstraction; Ari lands the product on the simplest
true sentence. The format is most effective when the intellectual frame is genuinely
interesting before Ari deflates it.

### Worst Possible Spokesperson

One host deliberately embodies the person least qualified to sell the product — wrong
context, wrong expertise, wrong energy, wrong everything. The other host corrects the
pitch and arrives at the actual message.

Protocol: the mismatch must be committed to, not half-hearted. The correction is not
the punchline — it is the product's real argument, stated clearly.

### Impossible Demonstration

A miniature visual scenario exaggerates the problem the product addresses — taken to
an absurd but logically coherent extreme. The scenario resolves into a factual landing
that states what the product actually does.

Protocol: the exaggeration must be internally consistent, not random. The factual
landing must be true and traceable to the sponsor's approved claims.

### Thirty-Second Film

A self-contained micro-scene, separately shot and inserted as a clearly labeled
commercial break. Not an interrupted interview — a produced short.

Protocol: labeled as a commercial break before and after. Shot separately from the
episode recording. May use the studio, or may be shot elsewhere. The creative brief
from the sponsor must include tone boundaries and factual requirements; execution
is controlled by the show.

### Sponsored Mechanic (Recurring)

A sponsor funds an existing repeatable segment that fits their product. The sponsor is
disclosed; the segment runs according to its normal editorial protocol; guests consent
explicitly to participation before the recording.

Protocol: editorial control, disclosure, and guest consent are all explicit and
documented before the recording. The sponsor cannot change the segment's structure.
If the fit is not genuine, the mechanic should not be offered to that sponsor.

## Disclosure and factual-control rule

- Every sponsored segment is clearly labeled as such before and during
- No invented personal use or invented results — if neither host has used the product,
  the script must not imply they have
- No guest is placed into a sponsored segment without prior documented consent
- Every factual claim made in any ad format must be traceable to the sponsor's approved
  claims document
- Premium ads are recorded separately from the interview; batch-shoot multiple
  variations in one session
- One clean, simple, direct version is always recorded alongside creative variations
  in case the sponsor needs low-risk copy for a specific placement
- The show retains the right to refuse any brief that requires invented claims or
  violates the dignity of the guest or the tone of the format

---

## Sponsor intake requirements

Every sponsor brief entering the Commercial Break Lab must contain:

- Product truth (one honest sentence about what the product does)
- Actual host experience, if any
- Required factual claims
- Prohibited claims
- Legal language and compliance requirements
- One clear call to action
- Visual permissions
- Tone boundaries
- Approval deadline

A brief that does not contain all of these items is returned before any creative work
begins. The intake requirements are not negotiable — they protect both the sponsor
and the show.
