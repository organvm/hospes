# Host's Morning Session

One session, roughly 12 minutes, once per week. This is the complete list of what requires Host. Everything else belongs to the producer and system.

HOSPES may classify and draft. It never sends, promises, books, or changes a protected relationship without the corresponding human gate.

---

## Before the clock — producer prepares the queue

The producer completes this before Host opens the digest:

- Show only synthetic or operator-authorized records in the queue.
- Verify that private contact data is outside Git.
- Attach source provenance to every proposed contact route and factual claim.
- Remove any card missing an owner, thesis, route, or explicit next action.
- Route legal, financial, sensitive, boundary, negative, representative, or unknown reply classifications to the human-review queue before the session starts.

If the queue is not ready, the session is shorter — not slower.

---

## The session — in order

### 0. Confirm the operating boundary (1 min)

Before any decisions: confirm out loud:

- The show and recording window under review.
- That no correspondence will be sent during this session.
- The producer who owns every resulting action.

**Receipt:** session scope, operator, producer, timestamp.

---

### 1. Confirm the one-sentence show promise (1 min)

Read the current audience promise in `dna/flagship.show.yaml` under `audience_promise`.

Ask: does this still describe the show you want to make?

If yes: nothing to do.
If no: rewrite it in one sentence. Hand it to the producer and system to update the DNA file. Do not rewrite the whole document — just the one sentence.

---

### 2. Triage the candidate stack (2 min)

The digest presents the top candidates in the approval queue. Each card shows:
- Guest name and field
- One-sentence episode thesis
- The claim the guest will defend
- Contact route and social cost
- Proposed artifact
- City

For each candidate, choose exactly one:

- **APPROVE FOR DRAFT** — the thesis is right, the claim is real, the fit is genuine; proceed to step 3.
- **REJECT** — the fit is wrong, the timing is bad, or the guest does not belong on this show right now.
- **PROTECT RELATIONSHIP** — this person is a friend or close contact. Do not approach without coming back to Host first, for any message, ever. A named relationship owner is required.
- **NEEDS RESEARCH** — the dossier is missing something needed to decide; write it as a note; producer owns the gap.

Do not infer relationship permission from a name, connection, or prior appearance.

Target: three decisions. If fewer than three candidates are ready, the system did not finish its work and you are done in less time.

Do not research or vet the candidates yourself. That is what the dossier is for.

**Receipt:** decision plus a short reason for each card.

---

### 3. Lock the editorial premise (3 min)

For each APPROVED FOR DRAFT candidate, verify:

- Why this guest and why now.
- The episode thesis.
- The claim to test.
- The proposed artifact.
- The source-backed contact route.

Move any unsupported premise to NEEDS RESEARCH — the producer owns the missing evidence.

**Receipt:** approved thesis and route provenance, or the missing-evidence owner.

---

### 4. Mark any protected relationships (1 min)

Scan the new-names column of the week's sourcing log (shown at the bottom of the digest).

If you see anyone who is a friend, close contact, or relationship that should never be approached without your personal sign-off — mark them PROTECTED RELATIONSHIP immediately.

Marking someone protected does not mean they will never be a guest. It means no message goes anywhere near them without you explicitly approving it, every time.

---

### 5. Add personal notes and boundaries (1 min)

- Record any personal note the producer should use for a specific candidate.
- Mark topics, promises, care needs, or commitments that must not be inferred.
- Keep private notes in their external owner. HOSPES may store only a bounded operational flag or
  opaque reference — never the note text, Git content, or a public artifact.

**Receipt:** policy flags and note-presence indicator. Never copy private note text into the receipt.

---

### 6. Confirm name-use permissions (30 sec)

The digest flags any draft where your name appears in the outreach (for example: "Host mentioned your name" or "Host asked me to reach out personally").

For each flagged draft: APPROVE (yes, my name can be used this way) or flag for producer review (something is off).

If your name is not in any draft this week, this step takes zero time.

---

### 7. Review drafts and reply exceptions (1–2 min)

- Review correspondence drafts for factual support, real sender identity, and one concise ask.
- Confirm that drafts claim no invented familiarity, praise, consumption, or mutual connection.
- Review reply classifications by evidence rule — without copying raw correspondence into this session.
- Every negative, ambiguous, representative, fee, legal, sensitive, or boundary reply must have a human owner before it leaves the queue.

**Receipt:** `DRAFT APPROVED`, `DRAFT REVISE`, or `NEEDS HUMAN`. This is not a send receipt.

---

### 8. Confirm recurring recording windows and studio routing (1 min)

Check the calendar summary at the bottom of the digest. It shows your blocked windows for the next six weeks.

If the windows are still accurate: nothing to do.
If something changed (travel, schedule shift, city change): mark the affected dates and the producer will update studio routing.

For interested guests, confirm proposed city: Los Angeles, New York City, Austin, or `UNROUTED`. Availability is proposed — not booked.

You do not negotiate specific session times. The producer handles that in the external booking
owner; HOSPES only tracks opaque booking evidence. You only confirm that your standing availability
windows are still correct.

**Receipt:** proposed city/window and any unresolved constraints.

---

### 9. Pick your natural segment role for this week's guest (30 sec)

Each approved guest has a segment card with a proposed role for you: instinct and friction, compression test (Explain It to Host), or skeptic chair (Opposite Chair).

Confirm or override. One word is enough: "friction is right" or "I want Opposite Chair instead."

If you do not override, the producer will default to instinct and friction.

---

### 10. Record one voice note and seal commitments (1 min)

**Voice note:** Pick one thing from this session that you want the system to remember — a personal
reaction to a candidate, a topic angle, an off-limits area, or a specific question. Record it as a
15–30 second voice note. The producer keeps the media and transcript in their external private
owner; HOSPES receives only a bounded flag or opaque reference. If nothing comes to mind, skip it.

**Seal:** Read back every promise, follow-up, and open commitment. Assign one next action and owner to each. Confirm the queue contains no unowned item and no implied send action. End the session — extra decisions wait for the next bounded review.

**Receipt:** commitment count, owner count, next review time, zero-unowned assertion.

---

## What does not belong in this session

The following items should never appear in Host's queue:

- Calendar negotiation with guests or studios
- Reply text decisions on received emails (triage is a system function; Host reviews exception-queue decisions only)
- Logistics (transportation, hotel, meal preferences)
- Follow-up tracking or reminder management
- Outreach copy review (that belongs to the producer)
- Sponsor copy review (that belongs to the producer)
- Publication scheduling decisions
- Social media copy decisions

If any of these appear in the digest, it is a system defect. Flag it to the producer.

---

## Completion predicate

The session is complete only when:

- Twelve minutes or less have elapsed.
- Every reviewed card has a decision or named blocker.
- Every consequential reply has a human owner.
- All external messages remain drafts — nothing was sent during the session.
- Every commitment has a next action.

If any condition is unknown, the session fails closed as `NEEDS HUMAN`.

---

## The rule

Everything else belongs to the producer and the system.

The session ends when the steps above are done. It should take less time each week as the system learns Host's preferences from accumulated decisions.
