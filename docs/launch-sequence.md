# Launch Sequence

## Doctrine

Do not start by burning the biggest relationship Host could possibly contact. Build evidence first,
then produce the artifact, then book the first public run, then — and only then — activate
selective marquee relationships. Each phase has a clear completion condition before the next begins.

## Phase 1 — Private Format Proof (Three Pilots)

Record three non-public pilots. Do not release them. They exist to prove the format, not to
generate an audience.

**Pilot 1:** A professionally familiar guest who will be comfortable — someone from Ring 1 or
Ring 2 (prior professional relationship or weak tie). No social cost to Host.

**Pilot 2:** A guest from one co-host's artistic, academic, literary, or technical world —
someone who establishes that the show can range beyond comedy.

**Pilot 3:** A weak-tie guest who does not owe either host anything — a genuinely cold or
near-cold booking through a representative or professional channel.

What these three episodes are testing:

- Whether the host contrast (instinct vs. architecture; compression vs. expansion) actually works
  in conversation
- Whether the three fixed segments (Claim, Stress Test, Artifact) feel organic
- Whether the Artifact produces anything worthwhile
- Whether hidden audio performs cleanly
- Whether the room reads as an intentional conversational environment on camera
- Which sections generate the strongest clips
- Whether guests report enjoying the experience

No pilot is wasted if it reveals a problem. A format flaw found in a non-public pilot costs
nothing. The same flaw discovered in Episode 3 of a public launch costs audience and relationships.

## Phase 2 — Artifact Production

Do not launch publicly until the following are complete:

- One 60–90 second sizzle (not a trailer that promises a show; evidence that the show already works)
- One one-page show brief (editorial premise, host functions, format engine, what makes it different)
- One one-page guest brief (what the booking experience entails; the guest proposition)
- Host biographies
- Three exemplary clips demonstrating the show's range across different guest types
- A professional booking page with recording calendar and contact form
- A concise and honest explanation of what makes the show different from other conversation
  programs

The artifact exists to close the gap between a representative's first read of the outreach and
their decision to say yes. Everything they need to make that decision is available without a
second email.

## Phase 3 — First Public Run (5–8 Guests)

Book the first public season through:

- Previous guest relationships from the *Unlicensed Therapy* archive (completed professional
  relationships, not friendship favors)
- Producers, publicists, managers — connector-class contacts
- Melrose-adjacent professional contacts already inside the studio ecosystem
- Weak ties with strong editorial fit
- Cold producer-led outreach for exceptionally strong editorial matches

Target: 5–8 confirmed guests before the first episode releases. Bank at least six strong
recordings before launching publicly. Practical launch structure: trailer, three episodes
demonstrating range, weekly release thereafter, clips and shorter assets between episodes.

The 85–90% figure applies here: the vast majority of first-season guests should not require
activating close personal relationships.

## Phase 4 — Selective Marquee Activation

After the format is proven, the sizzle is credible, and the first public run is underway:
approach one or two marquee relationships who are genuinely right for specific episodes.

The social proposition is no longer "help us launch this." It is: "We built something good, and
there is one conversation in it that feels specifically right for you." These are entirely different
asks. Burning a major relationship on Episode 1 while the format is still unstable is irreversible.
Using the same relationship to record a specifically designed episode inside a proven format honors
the relationship.

## Bank-6 / Drop-3 / Weekly

Record six strong episodes before the first drop. Release the first three in the launch window.
Release subsequent episodes weekly. The buffer exists to protect the show from production
disruptions and to allow retroactive editing decisions before any episode is public.

## Video-Native, Audio-Complete Doctrine

The show is video-native and must remain completely intelligible as audio. This is not optional
polish. The design conversation noted that YouTube reported more than one billion monthly active
viewers of podcast content in 2025, with podcast viewing on living-room devices exceeding 700
million hours in October 2025. Edison's 2026 research reported record podcast consumption and
found that 57% of Americans had both listened to and watched podcasts. Spotify reported that 40%
of users who discovered a new show in 2024 selected video, and its analysis found stronger
retention among smaller creators using video; Spotify explicitly recommends a "close your eyes"
test — the episode should still work as audio. Sources for these claims are registered in
[sources.md](sources.md) (APIs & platform guidance; podcast market research).

The no-visible-microphone, living-room visual grammar is not merely aesthetic. On large screens,
it makes the show read as a filmed encounter rather than a content studio recording. Every
recording produces: one complete episode, one strong trailer, three primary horizontal clips,
several vertical clips, chapter markers, episode-specific stills, one artifact asset, one guest
delivery package, a full audio edition, and a searchable description and transcript.

## Early-Metrics Table

Judge the show during the first season by these metrics, not by total views in Month 1:

| Category | Metric |
|---|---|
| Format | Audience retention at each segment transition |
| Audience | Returning viewers and subscribers per episode |
| Discovery | Clip viewers who reach the full episode |
| Booking | Reply rate and approval-to-recording time |
| Guest | Satisfaction, referrals, and fulfilled promises |
| Production | Time and cost per completed episode |
| Business | Sponsor interest, renewal, and revenue per recording |
| System | How often a human must correct the AI |

## The Central Early Question

The most important diagnostic question for the first season:

> Are people returning because of the show's mechanism and host dynamic, or only because they
> recognize a guest?

A show whose retention is driven entirely by guest fame is not a show yet — it is a guest-list.
A show whose audience returns for the mechanism, the host contrast, the segment format, and the
Artifact is a show. The launch sequence is designed to answer this question with data before any
marquee relationship is spent.
